Scope: the landing page (/). Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience and job: open source contributors arriving cold, now that the repo is public. Within one viewport they should understand that Spool sorts their scattered GitHub threads by whose move it is, and either connect a token or open the sample board.

Proof: the hero demonstrates the mechanism instead of describing it: a live sample bay hands strips between Your move and Their move, one sample-day event at a time. All demonstration strips are labelled SAMPLE; programs and fresh repos are live and labelled LIVE. No users, metrics or logos exist and none are shown.

Direction: The Flight Strip Bay (seed 04a12bd9). Memorable moments: a strip sliding from one bay to the other, the red pencil ring drawing round 19 days of silence, and a PR landing on MAIN in the 3D field while its amber branch folds back into main.

Sequence: hero (headline, lede, Connect GitHub strip, sample board, live bay), the field (3D airfield: PRs hold in review, get cleared and land as the merge; a deck with the phase rail, the strip being landed and a Landed rack), how a thread becomes a strip (pinned, prints one strip), a day at the bay (scrub clock), stalled (limit slider), callsigns, fresh repos (live), programs schedule beside Product Hunt (live), Google Summer of Code (live: the season route drawn by scroll with pickable stops and a project-size switch, tech bars that filter a searchable org rack), sign on (token form), footer strip you can type on.

Constraints: native scroll; one authored motion per section, all of it settled instantly by reduced motion or the motion switch (the field becomes a still you can step); dark by default with a light option; the field is the only 3D and the only place planes appear.

Open: whether the hero should also show a third bay (Stalled) once the sample day reaches a stall.
